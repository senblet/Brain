# Marketing — customer and competitor research

How to learn what customers need and how they say it, and how to research
competitors without fooling yourself: reading the audience, interviews,
reviews, confidence in findings, competitor profiles and win-loss. Used by
positioning ([content.md](content.md)), outbound
([outbound.md](outbound.md)) and product decisions
([product/strategy.md](../product/strategy.md)).

**Tags:** as in the [README](../README.md#how-notes-are-written). Practitioner
method from the `marketingskills` and `lenny-skills` repos; named where the
source attributes it.

---

## Before interviewing

- **Read the audience in public first** (Amy Hoy's "Sales Safari"): forums,
  reviews, communities. Collect their pains, jargon, recommendations and
  worldview — it tells you what to ask, and in whose words.
- **Recruit well:** customers with high deal size, short sales cycle and low
  churn first. End every call with "Who else should we talk to?"
- **How many:** aim for about 10 and accept 5 — themes repeat by the fifth
  or sixth call. For a product question, roughly 7–14.

## Interviews

- **Story, not opinion.** Open with "Tell me about the last time you…",
  follow the timeline, slow down at friction. No hypotheticals, no
  50-question scripts (Teresa Torres).
- **Ask for the trigger:** what made "today the day" they looked for
  something different (Bob Moesta).
- **DON'T pitch or defend the product.** Leave your solution out and see
  whether they raise the problem themselves. Write questions that could
  prove you wrong; ask "why" three to five levels down to the business
  outcome.
- Write opportunities as **unmet needs**, never as features, in a log kept
  apart from feature requests.
- The people who build also answer support and onboarding; keep a direct
  line (messages) to 5–10 motivated users.
- An agent may prepare questions and summarise; quotes stay verbatim and
  every claim cites its source ([ai/agents.md](../ai/agents.md#writing-a-task)).

## Reviews and other sources

- Read your own reviews in the order 3-star, 1-star, 5-star, 4-star; mine
  competitors' 4-star reviews for "I wish it could…".
- Allow for each source's bias: support tickets skew to problems;
  multiple-choice answers reflect the options offered.

## How sure is a finding?

- **High:** three or more independent sources, raised unprompted,
  consistent across segments. Medium and Low below that — label every
  insight.
- No persona from fewer than about five data points per segment.

## Competitor research

- **Keep three layers apart:** what you observed, what you infer, what it
  implies. Write "not observed on [pages], as of [date]", never "doesn't
  have"; never state a competitor's motive. Confirm a gap in their docs or a
  trial before using it publicly.
- Save raw captures in dated folders and never overwrite them; one capture
  can't support "hasn't changed".
- **Live ads are public:** the Meta Ad Library and Google Ads Transparency
  Center show every ad a company is running — channels, angles, formats, and
  how long the oldest have run (ads that run for months are usually
  working). Use them before a pitch or a positioning decision.
- **Competitors in three tiers:** same solution; different solution to the
  same problem; a conflicting approach or doing nothing (often the real
  alternative — a spreadsheet, a manual process).
- **Why people switch, or don't** — four forces: the push of the current
  problem, the pull of the new option, the habit of the old way, and anxiety
  about the change. Write down the anti-persona (who you don't serve) and the
  words to avoid; both go into the context file
  ([agents.md](agents.md#context-file-additions)).
- Comparison pages built from this research:
  [seo/content.md](../seo/content.md#page-types-programmatic-and-comparison-practitioner).

## Win-loss

- Include the wins, segment before counting, one primary reason per deal,
  and report sample sizes ("6 of 9 losses"). Under about five deals is a
  signal to watch, not a finding.
- CRM loss reasons skew towards price and timing; reread the notes or
  transcripts for the real reason and a condition that would reopen the deal
  ([agents.md](agents.md#which-agents-in-what-order)).
