# Marketing — content and brand

What to make, how to frame it, where to put it, and who makes it: brand
positioning, original insight, packaging, channels, and building a content
team. For writing pages that rank, see [seo/content.md](../seo/content.md);
for running content work with agents, see [agents.md](agents.md).

**Tags:** **MUST** / **SHOULD** / **DON'T** / **Skip** as in the SEO files.
All practitioner experience (read 2026-10-12), no data behind it; kept where
it is concrete and consistent with the rest of the brain.

---

## Positioning comes first

A brand needs product-market fit the way a product does. Before any content,
SHOULD answer in writing:

- Who exactly are we talking to?
- Why would they care — which problem do we solve for them?
- What can we say that no other brand can?
- How do they like to consume content (format, platform, length)?

The answers belong in the context file agents read
([agents.md](agents.md#the-context-folder-comes-before-any-agent)). Grow the
audience that buys, not views: reach on the wrong people is a vanity metric.

## Original insight is the moat

Content worth following says something only this brand can. Sources, roughly
from hardest to easiest to copy:

- proprietary data (your own numbers, usage, results);
- first-hand experience (what happened on real projects);
- access (people, places, situations others can't reach);
- frameworks (a named way of thinking about a recurring problem);
- contrarian takes, when they are honestly held and argued.

Same principle as the "one element the top results don't have" rule for
search pages ([seo/content.md](../seo/content.md#choosing-what-to-write-practitioner-method)).

## Packaging

Packaging — the title, thumbnail, hook or first line — is a separate skill
from the content itself. The same idea can flop or travel depending on how
it is framed. Good packaging:

- invites curiosity;
- promises a clear payoff, and the content delivers it;
- is specific;
- matches the platform's conventions.

**Search is different:** for a page meant to rank, the `<title>` and `<h1>`
summarise the page without exaggeration
([seo/appearance.md](../seo/appearance.md)). Curiosity-led framing is for
social posts, videos and newsletters, never a reason to bend a search title.

Copy that works is concise, easy to understand, and carries one strong idea.
Keep a swipe file of pieces that worked, with a line on *why*, and use it to
train taste — your own and your agents' (voice examples in
[agents.md](agents.md#the-context-folder-comes-before-any-agent)).

## Channels

- **Few platforms, done properly.** A good idea on the wrong platform still
  flops. Pick two or three where the audience actually is and learn how each
  one decides what to show. Long-form video skills transfer well to short
  form.
- **One channel, one job:** search answers questions, social starts
  conversations, email arrives at a specific moment. The same message on five
  channels pays five times for one impression.
- **Own a channel.** Collect contact details (newsletter, webinar sign-ups)
  so reach doesn't depend on one platform's algorithm.
- **Organic is the free test lab.** Post several hooks; put money only behind
  what already travelled.
- **Repurpose.** Repackage one strong idea across formats and platforms
  instead of starting every piece from zero.

## Testing creative

- Ask for genuinely different concepts (a worked example, a customer story,
  a raw demo, a founder take), not fifty headlines on one idea. Claims about
  how ad platforms group similar ads are a practitioner's and unverified; the
  practice holds regardless.
- AI actors are fine for testing scripts; pay a real person to run the
  winner — it performs better and keeps platform labelling rules simple.
- **UGC creators:** pick by views ÷ followers on recent videos, not by
  follower count. Small creators whose videos travel far past their own
  audience are the ones to brief. Watch their work before reaching out, then
  brief them with what the data showed and let them adapt it.

## Building a content team

- Early on one person covers several roles (strategy and voice, writing,
  design, video, community). Split them as volume grows; strategy, voice and
  the quality bar stay with one owner.
- **Hire for taste and thinking, train the skill.** Content is coachable but
  context-heavy; nobody arrives knowing the brand.
- SHOULD run a **paid test task** before hiring.
- Good candidates are often already posting: small accounts whose thinking
  fits the brand. Be public about the role.
- Be wary of someone leading content for the first time with no prior work
  next to a content lead. If you can't judge content yourself, borrow the
  judgement of someone who has hired for it.
- What makes the role attractive: stable pay, real creative control, a team
  rather than a one-person department, public credit, and room for their own
  brand.
- **Every recurring process gets a short written SOP** (research, hand-offs,
  who owns what). Draft it from meeting notes; a plain doc is enough.
- Set expectations on budget early: one content hire cannot be a whole
  marketing department.
