## Cold email — what moves reply rate

From Gong's analysis of 85 million cold emails, as published by 30 Minutes
to President's Club (seen secondhand as a chart, read 2026-10-12). These are
**correlations in one vendor's dataset**, not experiments; use them as
defaults to test, not laws.

| Fewer replies | | More replies | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buzzwords ("seamless", "best-in-class") | −57% | A breakup email ending the sequence | +89% |
| Case studies in a follow-up | −47% | Social proof | +41% |
| Asking for a meeting | −44% | Making an offer (free audit, trial, report) | +28% |
| The word "AI" | −36% | Restating the ask in a follow-up | +27% |
| Asking about their problem | −29% | Talking about their priorities | +20% |
| The word "platform" | −26% | "Thoughts?" as a follow-up | +9% |
| ROI claims | −17% | Asking if they're open to learning more | +7% |

Rules that follow:
- SHOULD lead with their priorities and a concrete offer; DON'T open with a
  meeting request or an ROI number.
- DON'T describe the product with buzzwords, "AI" or "platform" — say what
  it does.
- SHOULD end every sequence with a breakup email.
- Follow-ups restate the ask; they don't attach case studies.

Link-building outreach follows the same shape and adds "give something
verified first" — see [seo/content.md](../seo/content.md#earning-links-practitioner-method).

## Warm leads from competitor engagement (practitioner)

People who like or comment on a competitor's LinkedIn posts, or on posts by
its most active employees, have the category on their radar. Pick a few
competitors, follow their visible employees, check engagement daily, filter
to the ideal customer profile, and write a message that says why the product
is relevant. Engagement is not intent — it is a better starting point than a
purchased list, not a signal to pitch hard. Tools exist that automate the
monitoring; the source sells one.
