# SEO — local businesses

For a business that serves a city or area: the three places a local search
sends people, how the map results are ranked, the Google Business Profile,
reviews and citations, the website, regulated industries, and how to
measure. Everything in the other SEO files still applies to the website.

**Tags:** **MUST** = required for eligibility or a spam-policy line.
**SHOULD** = best practice with real effect. **DON'T** = actively harmful.
**Skip** = effort Google says is wasted. Items marked *(practitioner)* come
from local-SEO consultants, not Google, and are kept where they are
consistent with Google's guidance.

---

## Three results, three jobs

A local search ("botox near me", "divorce lawyer [city]") splits the page
into three areas, each with its own rules:

- **The map pack** — three businesses on a map, driven by the Business
  Profile. For "I need someone today" searches this is where the calls come
  from, and it can move within days once the work is right *(practitioner)*.
- **The regular results** — driven by the website, its content and links.
  Slower: expect movement over weeks.
- **AI answers** — they take clicks on question searches
  ([ai-search.md](ai-search.md)).

A business with a premises needs all three. One that sells online with no
storefront lives in the regular results and AI answers; if it also runs a
clinic or shop, that location gets its own profile.

## How the map pack is ranked

Google documents three factors:

- **Relevance** — how well the profile matches the search. Complete, detailed
  business information improves it.
- **Distance** — from the searcher to the business. Cannot be changed.
- **Prominence** — how well known the business is: links to it across the
  web, review count and ratings, and its position in web results. More
  reviews and positive ratings help; website SEO feeds this indirectly.

So the levers are the profile's completeness (relevance) and reviews plus
links (prominence). *(practitioner)* Most competitors filled their profile
once and never returned; completeness alone moves a profile.

## Choosing the searches *(practitioner)*

- Three kinds: **buying** ("DUI lawyer Phoenix", "lip filler near me"),
  **comparison** ("best med spa in [city]", "X vs Y") and **question** ("how
  much does a divorce cost"). Build for buying searches first, then
  comparison, then questions — questions build trust and feed AI answers but
  bring fewer direct clients.
- Collect them from Google's autocomplete, the "People also ask" box, and the
  services and locations the top three competitors' pages target; add volumes
  from a keyword tool. General method:
  [content.md](content.md#keywords-and-keyword-research).
- List each service the way people search it, including by the problem it
  solves ("botox [city]" and "forehead lines treatment [city]").
- With several services, start with the one with the most demand and the
  best margin.
- A young business SHOULD target suburbs and specific services first
  ("emergency plumber [suburb]") rather than "[service] [big city]".

## The Business Profile

- **Name** MUST be the real-world name as on the storefront and website — no
  added keywords or city names.
- **Address / service area** accurate and precise; service areas set to the
  places clients actually come from. Phone: a local number the business
  controls, not a call-centre line.
- **Categories:** Google says **use as few as possible**, as specific as
  possible, representative of the core business; skip any category redundant
  with a more specific one chosen; don't add one per product or service.
  Categories come from Google's fixed list — never invent one.
  *(practitioner)* The primary category is the most important field: pick the
  specific one for what you most want to be found for ("Divorce lawyer", not
  "Lawyer"; "Dermatologist", not "Medical clinic"). Practitioners push for
  several more; Google's wording wins — add one only when it is genuinely a
  core line of business. Agents invent categories that don't exist; paste the
  real list and have the model choose only from it.
- **Services:** each real service as its own entry with a short description.
  *(practitioner)* 20+ is common in a competitive city; only what you
  actually do.
- **Description:** useful information on services, products, mission and
  history. No prices, promotions or links of any kind.
- **Hours** *(practitioner)*: being open when the search happens helps;
  longer phone hours compete for evening searches. Only list hours you
  actually answer.
- **Photos, attributes, posts:** real photos of the place, team and work,
  added every month; every attribute that applies. *(practitioner)* 20+
  photos and one to three posts a week about the services you want more of.
  AI can draft a month of posts from real news and services; a human checks
  them.
- **Questions:** post the questions customers ask on first contact, with
  short clear answers, yourself.

## Reviews

- Ask at the moment the client is happiest (case closed, result visible,
  good follow-up); send a direct link, then one or two reminders over the
  next week. *(practitioner)* Ask them to mention the service and place —
  "handled my DUI case", "fixed our water heater same day in Providencia" —
  which helps for those searches.
- *(practitioner)* **Recent reviews outweigh the total**: 40 from the last
  three months often beat 300 from four years ago. Keep the flow steady.
- Reply to every review within about two days: short, warm, **no details of
  the client's case**. Negative reviews: calm, short, general; the owner
  reads before posting.
- **Health and legal businesses:** never confirm someone is a client or
  patient in a reply — a reply is public. A safe reply thanks them and
  invites them to call. (The source cites a US health-privacy fine against a
  dental practice for review replies naming patients' treatment.) Before and
  after photos need the patient's written consent.
- Build reviews on the industry's own platforms too, not only Google
  (legal, medical and aesthetics each have their own); AI assistants read
  them ([ai-search.md](ai-search.md)).
- DON'T buy or script reviews.

## Citations and links

- A citation is any listing of the business name, address and phone on
  another site. *(practitioner)* Inconsistent listings (an old address on
  some, a new one on others) weaken all of them. Search the name in quotes,
  list every listing, fix every wrong one, then add the missing directories.
- **Industry directories matter most**, and if you offer a branded
  product or treatment, get listed on the **manufacturer's "find a
  provider" page**.
- Good local links: professional associations, the chamber of commerce,
  local news, sponsorships of local events, a guest piece for a local
  publication on a question you answer every week. DON'T buy links
  ([content.md](content.md#earning-links-practitioner-method)).

## The website

### Structure *(practitioner)*
- **One page per service** — a single "Services" page listing fifteen
  treatments ranks for none of them. One page per case type, treatment,
  condition or product.
- **One page per location served**, with content specific to that place:
  where you file or practise, the neighbourhoods, parking, local reviews.
  Pages that only swap the town name are doorway abuse
  ([content.md](content.md#keywords-and-keyword-research)). Add suburb pages
  once the core pages rank, and write them about the place, not about you.
- Link each service page to its location pages and back, from inside
  paragraphs; keep important pages within about three clicks of the home
  page.
- The site mirrors the profile: one page per category, one per service.
  Write them in order of real search demand.

### The page the profile links to
Usually the homepage for a single-location business. *(practitioner)*:

- `<title>` and `<h1>`: primary category + city ("Home" as the title is free
  ground left on the table). Other pages: service + place + business name.
- Secondary categories as `<h2>` sections.
- Address and phone **character-for-character** as on the profile and every
  listing.
- `LocalBusiness` structured data, or the most specific subtype
  (`LegalService`, `MedicalClinic`, `Physician`…), with only true details and
  name, address and phone matching the profile; validate it
  ([appearance.md](appearance.md)). Skip FAQ markup and stars from your own
  reviews — Google no longer shows the first and doesn't allow the second
  ([appearance.md](appearance.md#structured-data)).
- A map embed of the profile and the Google reviews shown on the page.

### Built to convert *(practitioner)*
- On mobile: a tappable phone number at the top of every page, a short form
  or booking button above the fold.
- Fast on a phone — local searches happen on phones
  ([overview.md](overview.md#page-experience)).
- Ranking brings the visit; the page decides whether they call you or the
  next result.

## Regulated industries

Law, medicine, aesthetics and health products are "your money or your life"
topics, where Google expects more evidence of expertise
([content.md](content.md#e-e-a-t-and-ymyl)). On top of SEO, their claims
are regulated *(practitioner, check local rules)*:

- **Legal:** professional bodies often restrict words like "specialist" or
  "expert" and require disclaimers on results.
- **Medical and aesthetic:** no promised or guaranteed results; "most
  patients see results in two weeks" is a description, "guaranteed" is a
  liability.
- **Health products:** describe what the research shows, cite it, and make
  no claim that the product treats or cures anything; have claims checked
  by someone who knows the current rules.

## Measuring *(practitioner)*

- **What matters:** calls and form fills from Google every month; profile
  views, website clicks and direction requests; Search Console for pages.
- A **rank grid** samples the profile's position from points across the city
  (13×13 is common). Reduce it to one number: the share of points in
  positions 1–3 ("top-3 %"). Position 4 is page two of the map.
- Re-run monthly and after changes. Compare against the 3–4 businesses that
  dominate; their top-3 % is the ceiling. Below roughly a quarter to half of
  it, build pages that answer customers' questions; at or above it, build
  outward into the weak zones.
- **Timeline:** leads from the profile within days to a month; strong map
  positions around the office in roughly three months; very competitive
  cities closer to six. It compounds — stopping resets the clock.
